# Delayed learning evaluation

**Status: Designed; not conducted. No participants have been recruited and no learning improvement has been measured.**

## Research question and design

Estimate whether access to the redesigned practice experience changes delayed performance on comparable science questions relative to a comparison study activity covering the same material. This evaluates the whole experience; it cannot isolate a particular visual feature.

Recruit learners ages 8–15 through an approved process with guardian permission and child assent. Record age cohort (8–10, 11–13, 14–15), device and relevant accessibility needs only when necessary, using anonymous study identifiers. Provide an equivalent accessible format and an adult support route; do not collect names in the app or publish small, identifiable groups. Confirm the content, comparison activity, follow-up interval and consent/retention arrangements with educators and the study owner before recruitment.

## Procedure

1. Before assignment, administer a short, age-appropriate baseline assessment covering the selected science topics. Record the score and any prior exposure without giving answer feedback.
2. Allocate participants randomly within age cohort, or document the reason random allocation is infeasible. The intervention group practices with the redesigned app; the comparison group studies equivalent content for the same scheduled opportunity, using an agreed non-app method. Record actual exposure, assistance and interruptions without treating them as learning outcomes.
3. Use independently reviewed, equivalent question sets for baseline and follow-up; reserve delayed questions from the practice deck and use the same scoring rubric in both groups. Predefine accommodations and the procedure for learners who need them.
4. Administer the delayed assessment at a pre-registered common interval, proposed as 7 days, without practice prompts between sessions. Record actual elapsed time and any outside study. The study owner must approve the interval before testing.
5. Preserve an anonymized allocation log and report enrolled, allocated, assessed and analyzed counts for each group and cohort. State reasons for missing follow-up when known. Do not infer learning gains from points, clicks or time on screen.

## Analysis and reporting

Pre-register the primary outcome as the difference between groups in mean delayed percentage correct, adjusted for baseline score and age cohort where sample size supports it. Report both group means, the estimated difference and uncertainty interval, baseline balance, the number contributing to each result, missingness, deviations and limitations. Report cohort/device/accessibility breakdowns only when sample sizes and privacy safeguards allow. An initial small feasibility study should assess recruitment and measurement, not make a definitive efficacy claim; determine a confirmatory sample size from an explicit minimum meaningful difference, outcome variability, desired precision or power, expected attrition and available resources before running it.

## Results

Pending ethics and study-owner review, recruitment and testing. There are no observed baseline scores, comparison outcomes or delayed outcomes. No effectiveness claim is supported by the current implementation.
