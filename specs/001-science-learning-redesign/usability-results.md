# Learner and adult usability evaluation

**Status: Prepared; not conducted. No participants have been recruited and no usability outcomes are claimed.**

## Recruitment and protection

With appropriate guardian permission and child assent, recruit learners in age cohorts 8–10, 11–13 and 14–15 across devices, reading abilities and accessibility needs; include parents and teachers. Aim for at least five learners per age cohort for an initial directional iteration, then recruit enough participants to interpret the specification's percentage targets responsibly. Five-person samples are not representative and cannot establish population-level success. Use assigned study IDs and safe fictional nicknames; do not collect real names in the product or publish identifying combinations of characteristics.

## Moderated task protocol

Use the same neutral task prompts and a test deck containing five questions for each participant. Ask learners to:

1. Enter using a safe nickname and explain who may see it before proceeding.
2. Find and begin a science deck without assistance; record time from the entry screen to the first question.
3. Complete five questions, including a prepared incorrect response, read the feedback and find the review for that missed answer.
4. Locate the optional rankings, then continue without viewing them; describe what they would do if a session ended or a save could not be confirmed.
5. Rate ease and confidence on an age-appropriate five-point scale after the tasks, without prompting for a favorable response.

Ask adults to create a nickname-only profile, identify the public/private ranking setting before saving, locate the device-link and revocation controls, and find private deck and file management. Ask them to explain the visibility and deletion effects. Record errors, pauses, requested help and verbatim observations without recording sensitive data. If accessibility support is requested, document the assistive technology or accommodation with participant consent.

## Observation and reporting template

For each task record participant ID, cohort or adult role, device category, relevant accessibility support if volunteered, independent success (yes/no), time where applicable, assistance given and observed barriers. Predefine independent success as completion without facilitator instruction or correction; note incomplete tasks and technical failures separately. Report numerators and denominators, not just percentages, for each cohort and outcome, and distinguish confidence/ease ratings from task performance. The targets in SC-001–SC-006 are proposed thresholds, not observed results. Avoid publishing breakdowns where small groups could identify participants.
